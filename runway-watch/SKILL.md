# Runway watch

**The aim:** the founder always knows the number, and hears about it *when it changes*, not in a
monthly report they have to open.

## Getting the number

Runway is cash ÷ net monthly burn. Both parts come from connected systems — bank balance, payroll,
billing — never from the founder's memory. If a figure cannot be pulled, **say which one is missing
rather than estimating around it.** A runway number built on a guessed burn is worse than no number,
because it will be believed.

Use the trailing three months for burn, not last month alone. One large annual invoice makes a
single month meaningless, and a founder spooked by a one-month spike stops trusting the whole thing.

## When to speak up

Not every week. Say something when:

- runway crosses a threshold that changes behaviour — **12, 9, 6, 3 months**
- burn moves more than ~20% versus the trailing average
- the direction reverses — burn was falling and is now rising

Otherwise stay quiet. **A watch that reports "no change" every week gets muted, and is then worth
nothing on the week that matters.**

## How to say it

Lead with the number and the delta: *"Runway is 9.4 months, down from 11.2 last month — burn rose to
$84k from $71k."* Then the cause, if it is visible in the data.

**Do not offer reassurance and do not editorialise.** "Still healthy!" is not yours to say — the
founder knows their fundraising position and you do not. Give them the number and the cause; the
judgement is theirs.

If the cause is a one-off — an annual bill, a bonus cycle — say so plainly. That is the difference
between a useful alert and a panic.

## What to record

After each check, write the date, cash, burn and computed runway to memory. That history is what
lets the next check say *"down from"*, and it is what makes the number trustworthy over time.
