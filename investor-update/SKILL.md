# Investor update

**The aim:** the founder reviews a draft that is already right, instead of starting a blank
page every month. This job is dreaded because it is *assembly* — pulling numbers, recalling
what was promised, writing it in a voice — and assembly is the part to remove.

**Never send it.** Draft, then stop and ask. An investor update is outbound, irreversible,
and speaks for the company. It goes out on a human's click, always.

## What the draft is made of

1. **The numbers, from the source.** Pull them; do not ask the founder to type them in. Use
   the connected systems they already have — revenue and churn from billing, usage from the
   product database, pipeline from the CRM. Every figure must trace to something you read.
2. **What you said last time.** Search memory for the previous update before writing. The
   most valuable line in any update is *"last month we said we would do X"* — followed by
   whether it happened. Investors are tracking that whether or not you mention it.
3. **What actually happened.** Shipped work, hires, customers won and lost, anything that
   moved the plan.
4. **The ask.** Most updates end with one — intros, candidates, advice. Ask what it is if
   there isn't an obvious one; do not invent it.

## Writing it

Use the founder's voice — apply `email-voice-profile` if one exists. Short, plain, and
specific. Investors read dozens of these; ornament reads as noise, and confidence without
numbers reads as evasion.

**Lead with the number that matters most this period, good or bad.** A hard number stated
plainly buys more credibility than a good number buried under three paragraphs of framing.

**Do not soften a bad month.** If revenue fell, say it fell, then say why and what changes.
An update that hides a decline destroys trust exactly when it is most needed, and the
founder is the one who pays that bill later — not you.

## Rules

- **A number you could not pull is a gap you name**, never a number you estimate. Write
  "couldn't reach Stripe — this figure is missing" and keep going.
- **Never invent a metric, a customer name, or a quote.** Everything traceable or absent.
- Flag anything that looks wrong before it is sent — a 10x jump is usually a broken query,
  not a miracle, and it is far cheaper to check than to correct in the next update.
- Keep confidential detail (individual salaries, unannounced departures, live M&A) out
  unless the founder puts it in.

## After approval

Record in memory: the period covered, the headline numbers, the commitments made, and the
ask. Next month's draft starts by checking that list — that is what makes the second update
better than the first.
