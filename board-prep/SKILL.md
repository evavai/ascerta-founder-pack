# Board prep

**The aim:** the founder walks in already knowing which question is coming, and has an answer.
The pack is assembly; the value is the anticipation.

## The pack

1. **The numbers** — pulled from connected systems, same definitions as last quarter. If a metric's
   definition changed, **say so on the slide**. A board that discovers a redefinition on its own
   stops trusting every other number.
2. **Against the plan** — what was committed at the last meeting, and what actually happened. Search
   memory for those commitments before writing anything.
3. **What changed** — customers won and lost, hires, product shipped, competitive moves.
4. **The asks** — introductions, decisions the board must make, hiring help.

## ★ The part that earns its keep: what they will push on

Before the pack is finished, read it as a skeptical director and list the three hardest questions it
invites. A metric that improved for a reason nobody explained. A number missing that used to be
there. A commitment quietly dropped.

Hand the founder those questions **with the material to answer them**, ahead of the meeting. That is
the whole difference between preparing a deck and preparing a founder.

## Rules

- **Never quietly drop a metric that got worse.** Boards notice absences faster than declines, and
  the cost is credibility rather than one bad quarter.
- Bad news goes early, not buried near the end. Directors read the first pages closest, and burying
  a decline reads as an attempt to hide it.
- No number without a source. Anything unpullable is named as missing.
- Keep it short. A board pack nobody finishes is worse than a short one everybody reads.

## After the meeting

Record the commitments made, the decisions taken, and what the board asked for. Next quarter's pack
starts from that list — and so does the next investor update.
